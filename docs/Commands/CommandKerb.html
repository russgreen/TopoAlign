Sweep Kerb

Sweeps a profile along picked edges or model lines to make a kerb. The profile always stands vertical, however the edge rises or falls, and corners are mitred so the kerb keeps its true width. The kerb is made as a direct shape or as a loaded family, in the Roads, Hardscape or Generic Models category.

Usage / Steps

  1. Launch the command. Model lines that are already selected are picked up for you.
  2. Click Pick edges and select the edges of a toposolid, floor, road or any other element, or click Pick model lines and select model lines or arcs. You can pick more than once; picks are added together. Clear picks starts again.
  3. Choose the kerb profile. Profile families come from the project, for example from Revit's Profiles library.
  4. Set the options and click Create Kerb.
  5. With Show the kerb and ask before keeping it ticked (the default) the kerb is built in the model and selected, and you choose Keep the kerb, Flip to the other side (it is rebuilt on the other side and you are asked again) or Cancel, which leaves nothing behind. The side you end on is remembered for next time.

Options / Parameters

  • Kerb profile — a closed shape of lines and arcs drawn in a profile family. The family's origin sits on the edge; X runs across the kerb and Y runs up.
  • Show the kerb and ask before keeping it — builds the kerb in the model, selected, before it is committed, so you can see which side the profile is on and flip it.
  • Flip profile left to right — the profile's positive X goes to the right of the direction of travel. A run keeps the direction of the first edge picked, and a closed loop goes anticlockwise so the right-hand side is the outside. Tick this to put the kerb on the other side.
  • Section spacing — the longest distance between sections on a curved edge. Straight edges only get a section at each end.
  • Vertical offset — raises or lowers the whole kerb relative to the edges.
  • Category — Roads, Hardscape or Generic Models, as far as your version of Revit offers them.
  • Create as — a direct shape, or a family loaded into the project with a type you can see in the Project Browser.

Output / Results

  • One element per run: a direct shape, or an instance of a new family named "TopoAlign Kerb - ...".
  • Edges that join end to end make one continuous kerb; edges that don't touch make separate runs in the same element.
  • Each use creates a new kerb. Nothing is replaced; use Undo to remove one.

Tips & Pitfalls

  • Edges must meet to within about 0.1 mm to be joined into one run. No more than two edges may meet at a point.
  • A corner sharper than about 130 degrees is not mitred: the kerb is cut in two there with square ends.
  • A completely vertical edge can't be followed by a vertical profile.
  • The profile must be narrower than the tightest curve on its inside. Where it is not, Revit can't make that span and the kerb has a gap.

Troubleshooting

  • The picked edges branch → Pick one run of edges at a time.
  • No profile families are loaded → Load one from Revit's Profiles library, or draw your own closed shape in a profile family.
  • The kerb is on the wrong side → Choose Flip to the other side when asked, or tick Flip profile left to right in the dialog.
  • Revit could not build any geometry → The profile may be too wide for a curve or corner, or may overlap itself.
